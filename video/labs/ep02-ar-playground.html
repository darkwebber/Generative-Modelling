Episode 02 · playground

Build an autoregressive model with your hands

A character-level n-gram model, trained by counting, running live in your browser. Change the knobs, step through the generation loop, force letters yourself, and watch the probabilities respond.

1 · the knobs

Context length is how many previous letters the model sees. Temperature τ reshapes the distribution before sampling.

2 · training data

Any list of words works — one per line or space-separated. Only a–z is kept.

3 · the generation loop, one step at a time

Press step: the model looks at the last k letters, shows p(next | context), then rolls the dice. Click any bar to force that letter instead and see how the context changes the next distribution.

context:
 
 
4 · sample many

Twenty fresh samples. Grey ones already exist in the training data: the model memorised them.

5 · how surprised is the model?

Type any name. Each bar is −log p of that letter given the ones before it. Their sum is −log p(name).

6 · the bigram count table

Row = previous letter (down the left), column = next letter (along the top), brightness = count N(a, b). “.” is the start/end of a name. Hover or tap a cell to read it. This table is the k = 1 model.

 
The same model in ~20 lines of Python is in code/ar_counting.py; a neural version is in code/ar_neural.py.
