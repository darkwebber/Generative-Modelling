Diffusion Lab: noise in, digits out

The real diffusion network from the video runs in your browser: 2.7 million weights, trained to predict the noise in an MNIST digit at any of 1,000 noise levels. Watch it pull digits out of static step by step, see what it believes at each step, morph between noise seeds, and let it redraw your own scribbles.

Each step: predict the noise ε_θ(x_t, t), form the guess x̂₀ = (x_t − √(1−ᾱ)·ε_θ)/√ᾱ, and move to the next noise level. DDPM also adds a little fresh noise at every step.

view: x_t shows the noisy images as they clean up. view: x̂₀ shows the network’s guess of the finished digit at that moment: a blurry average early on, a specific digit soon after, details last.

The same model in Python: code/diffusion.py, exported by code/export/export_ep09_assets.py (weights stored in 8 bits here). Net: [x (784), sinusoidal embedding of t (64)] → 1024 → 1024 → 784, swish, t fed into every hidden layer; the output is its guess x̂₀ (through tanh) and the noise is read off it, ε_θ = (x_t − √ᾱ·x̂₀)/√(1 − ᾱ). Linear β from 0.0001 to 0.02 over 1,000 steps.
